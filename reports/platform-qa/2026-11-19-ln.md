### 1. One failed read of the trainee's record wipes their progress  — high
Where: `index.html:577-594` (`loadTrainee`), `:595-600` (`saveTrainee`); also `:583` and `:929-936` for `progress:<id>`   Category: Bug (data loss)

What happens (plain): when the page opens, it reads the trainee's record. If that one read fails, the page treats them as brand new and
saves an empty record over their real one. Their finished days, the Desk's Day 2 / Day 3 scores, and their name are gone, in LN and on the
Portal's progress pages.
1. A trainee who has finished Day 1 reloads the course.
2. The `/api/storage/get` for `trainee:<id>` fails once: a 500 from the Worker, or a network blip.
3. `loadTrainee` catches the error, sets `state.trainee = { id, name: id, dayProgress: {} }`, and calls `saveTrainee()`.
4. 300 ms later that empty record is written. The Worker keeps only `approved/rejected/archived/registeredAt`.

Verified (Playwright, one routed 500): before, `{"1":{"modules":{"d1-deck":true,"d1-video":true},"step":3,"done":true,…}}`. After,
`{}`, and `name` became `wipe-test--b100926`. `progress:<id>` has the same pattern. A failed read gives `state.progress = {}`, and the
next answer typed or Desk change saves `{}` plus that one change over every saved answer and the whole Lien Desk case.

+  try{ state.trainee = await kvGet("trainee:" + state.traineeId); }
+  catch(e){ toast("Couldn't load your progress. Reload the page before you go on."); state.trainee = state.trainee || { id: state.traineeId, name: state.traineeId, dayProgress: {} }; return; }

-  try{ state.progress = (await kvGet("progress:" + state.traineeId)) || {}; }catch(e){ state.progress = {}; }
+  try{ state.progress = (await kvGet("progress:" + state.traineeId)) || {}; }
+  catch(e){ toast("Couldn't load your work. Reload the page before you go on."); state.progress = {}; return; }
+  loadedOk = true;

### 2. The Net Sheet "Check" gives the right figure for every line, so the Day 2/3 Net Sheet points can be earned without opening a document  — high
Where: `js/ln-desk.js:553` (`vSheet` → `res`: "`${money(i.got)}, the file says ${money(i.want)}`"), `:266-287` (`checkSheet`, unlimited),

- The blank check showed "Settlement amount: $0.00, the file says $25,000.00 … Total expenses … the file says $208.70 … Mercy General
  Hospital … no row for it …".

- On the third check: **"✓ Your sheet ties out"**. `documents viewed: []`. Grade: "The initial Net Sheet ties out **40/40**".

"…, the final figure is $X" for each payee (`csIssues`, `:350-358`). The Reduction Request coach prints the current balance and the PIP
paid (`rrValid`, `:342-343`). So the README's line "The answer keys themselves are not in this public repository" holds only for the original
activity keys. The page itself computes and shows every expected figure, and `LNDesk.expected('final')` returns all of them from the console.

`why`: it is the actual teaching ("the PIP log, not the ledger").

## Learning features (ranked)

Negotiating a reduction is the core skill of this job, and the Lien Desk is the right place to practise it. Right now, though, every
reduction is one email with one fixed answer: ask at or above the provider's floor and they accept, ask below and they counter at the
floor (`ln-desk.js:441-442`). That counter is then recorded as agreed without the trainee doing anything (`w.got.approved[p.id] = approved`).
So asking **$0.00 on every bill always lands the best possible figure, at no cost**, and the reduction letter carries no argument. The first
four ideas below fix that.

### 1. Real negotiation rounds: counter, justify, accept  — effort M
**What:** when a provider counters, the trainee decides what to do next: accept, counter again with a reason, or escalate to the attorney.
The provider may move again, up to its hidden floor, depending on the reason and the number of rounds.
**Why it helps the trainee:** today the dominant move is "ask $0" and the negotiation is over in one email, so the Desk never trains the
real judgment: the opening ask, what to concede, and when to stop. The Net Sheet and Closing Statement then take whatever figure the
simulator set.
**Where:** `respond()`'s reduction branch in `js/ln-desk.js`. Give each payee's `reduce` an `open` (its first counter), a `floor` and a
`steps` list. Record `w.got.offer[p.id]` until the trainee replies "accept" in the thread, and only then set `approved`. Scoring goes in
`grade("day3")`: points for landing within X% of the floor in 2 rounds or fewer, and fewer for a $0 opening ask to a provider whose
policy never waives. Reuse the existing inbox, Reply and coach lines.
**Dependencies:** new fields in `ln-desk-cases.js` (12 cases) and a grade-rule change. No KV or secrets.

### 2. Reduction letters that have to argue: hardship, pro-rata costs, policy limits  — effort S/M
**What:** the Reduction Request draft gets the reasons a negotiator actually cites (client hardship, limited policy limits, pro-rata
share of attorney fees and costs, the provider was already paid by PIP). The provider's answer depends on whether the right reasons were
given for this case.
**Why it helps the trainee:** the draft form checks file name, client, DOL, PIP, balance and amount, but never why the provider should
reduce. The code already half-has this: `LNDesk.field` handles `data-r` reason checkboxes (`ln-desk.js:658`), but no form renders them and
the letter never prints them.
**Where:** `vDrafts` (render the reason checkboxes from a `REASONS` list), `docRR` (print them), and `respond()` (each case's `reduce.best`
reasons decide the first counter). It pairs with feature 1.
**Dependencies:** a `best` list per payee in `ln-desk-cases.js`.

### 3. "Try it yourself first": hide the answer until the trainee has had a go  — effort S
**What:** the Net Sheet, Closing Statement and Reduction Request checks say which lines are wrong and why ("use the PIP log, not the
ledger"), but give the correct figure only on a "Show me" press that costs that line's credit.
**Why it helps the trainee:** today the first Check prints every answer (finding 2), so a trainee learns to click Check instead of reading
the bill, the PIP log and the payoff letter. A miss with a reason sends them back to the document, which is where the learning happens.
**Where:** `vSheet` `res()`, `csIssues`, `rrValid` and `ck()` in `js/ln-desk.js`. This is the fix for finding 2, built as a feature.
**Dependencies:** none (a `reveal` map in the existing `progress:<id>` desk state).

### 4. A short Knowledge Check after the Day 1 and Day 2 lessons  — effort M
**What:** 8–10 scenario questions after each day's deck and video, each with its reason shown after answering. For example: "An ERISA
self-funded plan asserts a lien: can state anti-subrogation law reduce it?" or "Medicare's conditional payment letter vs. the final demand:
which one goes on the final Net Sheet?"
**Why it helps the trainee:** Days 1 and 2 (19 topics: liens, ERISA and government-plan liens) are watched, not tested. A module counts
as done when the trainee presses Next (`recordStep`, `index.html:639-653`), so nobody, trainer included, knows whether the rules sank in
before the trainee negotiates on Day 2–3. The per-question reason also replaces Day 3's keyword grader as the knowledge measure (finding 3).
**Where:** a new `js/ln-kc.js` step kind `quiz` in `ln-course.js`, modelled on PD's Knowledge Check (75 questions with rationales, best
score kept, every attempt logged). Write the score to `dayProgress[n].score`, which the Portal already reads.
**Dependencies:** question content from the program owner. No new keys.

### 5. "What you saved the client": the negotiation result, shown as money  — effort S
**What:** a card on the Desk's Tasks tab and on Day Complete: "Your reductions cut $X from $Y in balances (Z%). Your client takes home
$N instead of $M."
**Why it helps the trainee:** the figures are already computed (`expected(c, w, "initial")` vs `"final"`), but a trainee sees only
pass/fail lines and a 0–100 score. Framing the work as the client's money is the purpose of the role, and it makes a better ask visibly
worth more, which rewards feature 1's skill directly.
**Where:** `vTasks` and `gradeCard` in `js/ln-desk.js`, and the `done` step in `index.html` `viewPlayer`. Admin's Lien Desk table can show the
same figure per trainee.
**Dependencies:** none.

### 6. A practice case before the scored one, with the demo's John Doe as a worked example  — effort S
**What:** before their own case, the trainee works John Doe (the demo's case, `johndoe` in `ln-desk-cases.js`) unscored, alongside the
demo recording. Afterwards they can open any other of the 12 cases to practise again.
**Why it helps the trainee:** each trainee gets exactly one case (`caseId()` picks it from their id, or the trainer assigns it), so the
first Lien Desk attempt is also the graded one. A mistake can't be practised away on a fresh file. The Tutorial Demo walks through John Doe,
but the trainee can't then do John Doe themselves with the coach on.
**Where:** `caseId()` and `W()` in `js/ln-desk.js` already keep each case's work separately (`desk.cases[id]`). Add a case picker marked
"Practice (not scored)", and have `keepGrade` read only the assigned case.
**Dependencies:** none.

### 7. Trainer's daily feedback from LN's Admin, shown on the Portal  — effort M
**What:** in 🛡 Admin, a trainer writes each trainee's day rating and notes next to the Desk score and the answers. The trainee sees it on
their dashboard.
**Why it helps the trainee:** the Portal's Trainee Monitoring already reads `ln:feedback:<id>` (`program-progress.js`, the same shape
FT/CM use), but LN never writes it. Today a trainer's coaching on a negotiation lives in chat and is lost. The Desk summary (emails, sheet
checks, grade) is already on the Admin row, so the feedback form can be pre-filled with "Initial sheet 9/10 at last check; 2 replies asked
to fix and resend".
**Where:** a new `js/ln-feedback.js` (Admin form + trainee card). The Worker needs `feedback:<id>` readable by its own trainee (`canRead`).
**Dependencies:** none new. `feedback` is already in `_purge.js` `FAMILIES`.

### 8. Phone negotiation with a billing office on the training phone  — effort L
**What:** a Ring Channel call pack where the trainee phones a provider's billing office to request an updated balance and argue a
reduction, scored on the call scorecard.
**Why it helps the trainee:** much real lien work happens by phone, and the Portal's Call Simulator is built for it, but LN uses none
of the Portal's simulators. The Email Workspace already has a "Billing & Liens" label (`simulators/email-packs.js`) that could carry
LN scenarios as a quicker first step (effort M).
**Where:** a Ring Channel pack (lshringchannel repo), `launch.js` `CALL_PROGRAMS` + `sim-results.js` `COURSES` gain `LN: 'ln:'`, and an LN
"Graded calls" card reading `ln:callsim:<id>` (copy PD's `js/graded-calls.js`).
**Dependencies:** new `ln:callsim:` / `ln:simresults:` keys (on the contract, so revoke covers them), and AI scoring cost per call.

### 9. A lien glossary: tap "subrogation", "made-whole", "common fund"  — effort M
**What:** the legal and billing terms on the Desk and the Process Questions (subrogation, ERISA, made-whole doctrine, common-fund
reduction, pro-rata, PIP, MedPay, conditional payment, payoff) get a one-line definition on tap.
**Why it helps the trainee:** these terms appear in the documents (`docLien`, `docPip`), in the coach lines and in the 12 questions, but no
page defines them. Day 1's deck is the only source.
**Where:** a `js/ln-glossary.js` that wraps known terms in `.ld-doc` iframes' text and in `qa-list`. Ideally it shares definitions with the
Portal's proposed simulator glossary (portal report, feature 7).
**Dependencies:** definitions from the program owner.
